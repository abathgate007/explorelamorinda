Lamorinda is not one uniform housing market. Lafayette, Moraga, and Orinda differ by neighborhood, topography, transit access, housing stock, school-district boundaries, and current inventory. General guides are useful for orientation; property decisions require current, address-specific information.

## Buyer Priorities

Before relying on a listing description or neighborhood summary, verify:

- financing and the full monthly carrying cost;
- homeowner-insurance availability and pricing for the exact address;
- natural-hazard and seller disclosures;
- attendance assignment directly with the relevant school district;
- drainage, foundation, roof, sewer, electrical, and other inspection needs appropriate to the property;
- permits and records with the relevant jurisdiction;
- current comparable sales rather than an undated price range.

CAL FIRE's [Fire Hazard Severity Zone information](https://osfm.fire.ca.gov/what-we-do/community-wildfire-preparedness-and-mitigation/fire-hazard-severity-zones) explains that its maps classify hazard, not an individual property's insurance outcome. The California Department of Insurance explains that insurers use their own underwriting and wildfire models. Obtain an insurance quote early.

## Seller Priorities

A seller's plan should be based on the property and current competing inventory. Common early tasks include:

- reviewing required disclosures with qualified professionals;
- deciding which inspections are appropriate before marketing;
- confirming permit and property records;
- prioritizing repairs and presentation work;
- reviewing recent comparable sales and active competition;
- documenting a pricing and launch strategy.

Do not assume that a renovation, inspection, or specific launch month has the same return for every property.
