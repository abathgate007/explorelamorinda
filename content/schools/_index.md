Lamorinda is served by three K-8 districts and one regional high-school district. Schools are an important part of many housing decisions, but rankings, programs, staffing, enrollment rules, and attendance boundaries can change.

## Start With the Official District

| Area | K-8 district | High-school district |
|---|---|---|
| Lafayette | [Lafayette School District](https://www.lafsd.org/) | [Acalanes Union High School District](https://www.acalanes.k12.ca.us/) |
| Moraga | [Moraga School District](https://www.moraga.k12.ca.us/) | [Acalanes Union High School District](https://www.acalanes.k12.ca.us/) |
| Orinda | [Orinda Union School District](https://www.orindaschools.org/) | [Acalanes Union High School District](https://www.acalanes.k12.ca.us/) |

Use the [California School Dashboard](https://www.caschooldashboard.org/) to review current state-reported measures. When comparing third-party ratings, check the data year and methodology rather than treating a letter grade or percentile as permanent.

## Verify Before Relying on an Assignment

A street address, not a town name alone, is the appropriate starting point for enrollment research. District residency requirements and attendance boundaries control; a real-estate listing, map pin, or informal description does not.

Before making a housing decision:

1. Contact the relevant K-8 district with the exact property address.
2. Confirm the applicable high-school district and assigned campus.
3. Ask whether boundary, capacity, transfer, or enrollment policies affect the assignment.
4. Save the district response and note the date.
5. Reconfirm if significant time passes before enrollment.

The [Orinda Union School District residency guidance](https://www.orindaschools.org/Enrollment/Residency-Verification/index.html) and [Moraga School District enrollment resources](https://www.moraga.k12.ca.us/) illustrate why current residency documentation matters.

## How to Compare Schools Thoughtfully

Test scores and graduation rates describe only part of a school. Families may also want to compare:

- course and enrichment offerings;
- student support and special-education services;
- transportation and daily logistics;
- campus size and transition points;
- extracurricular programs;
- current enrollment and class-size information;
- policies that matter to a student's individual needs.

Use consistent data years when comparing districts. If a figure cannot be tied to a current primary source, treat it as provisional.
